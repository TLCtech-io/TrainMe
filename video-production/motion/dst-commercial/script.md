# DST commercial: script and storyboard (draft 1)

A 30-60 s commercial for the TLC_TRNG Decision Support Tool, for a conference booth loop and the website. 16:9,
narrated with a Descript AI voice. The facts come from `fact-brief.md` (from the DST Code session, release 3.3) and
the public pages (tlctrng.com/dst and the demo's overview PDF). Terms follow the brief's Use and Avoid lists.

**Built to work muted.** Booth screens usually play without sound, so the on-screen text tells the whole story by
itself. The narration adds detail for the website. The end card fades back into the opening shot, so the booth
version loops cleanly.

---

## 1. Who it's for, and the problem

The brief's versions, tightened. These are for the narration and the website.

- **Who it's for:** the people who make the call when operations are disrupted. That means executives, emergency
  managers and duty officers at ports, utilities, local governments and other organizations that run critical
  operations.
- **The problem:** when several things fail at once, every leader asks the same question: what do we fix first? The
  answer is already in their continuity plans. But a plan in a binder or a spreadsheet can't give it to them in the
  moment.
- **The answer, in one line:** the DST turns those plans into a ranked restoration order and a leadership brief,
  in minutes, on any device.
- **Tagline:** *The DST ranks. You decide.* This restates the brief's own rule (it ranks and shows consequences;
  people decide), and it rules out "AI" or "automated decisions" readings up front.

## 2. The demo path for the spot

The brief's nine-step walkthrough and the story I'd sketched from the public pages are the same arc. For 30-60 s it
gets trimmed to the moments that read on screen:

1. Open on the map, not the sign-in screen or Home. The storm and the facilities are the hook.
2. Draw the impact area, and add what's inside to a scenario.
3. The ranked restoration order, then the cascade.
4. One what-if: Disrupted to Manual Ops, and the order changes.
5. The report, then sharing it from a phone.

Left out to save time:
- Sign-in and Home. Home could be a one-second shot in the 60 s cut.
- Opening a single asset's details. Its 21 / 19 / 40 dependency numbers are strong B-roll if a beat needs filling.

## 5. Look

- **Frames:** desktop shots sit in a clean browser frame, and phone shots in an iPhone frame, like the screenshots
  on tlctrng.com/dst.
- **Color:** navy, amber and mustard to match the tool, and the TLC_TRNG brand design system for type and the end
  card.
- **Demo data tag:** a small "Demo · simulated data" note in a lower corner of every shot of the tool.
- **The URL** stays on screen only. Spoken aloud, "tlctrng.com slash dst" is a mouthful.

## 6. For the DST Code session to fact-check

Please confirm or correct each line as it is worded:

1. "Your plans hold the answer": a real build is made from the organization's continuity plans (assets,
   dependencies, contacts).
2. "Every facility inside joins a scenario in one tap" (the **Add N to scenario** button).
3. "Ranks them by Restoration Score, built from your own priorities and dependencies."
4. "Total Cascade shows everything that goes down with them."
5. "Switch an asset to Manual Ops and watch the order change": does the order visibly change in the demo scenario
   we'd use? If not, which change does move it?
6. "A Scenario Impact Report: critical questions and initial actions first, shared right from your phone."
7. "Brief leadership in one tap": fair shorthand for Share Summary, or should it be "in a few taps"?
8. "The DST ranks. You decide."

## 7. Open items before capture

- **Pronunciation:** how is TLC_TRNG said aloud ("T-L-C Training"?), for the Descript AI voice? DST is said "D-S-T".
- **The phone's share sheet:** my browser can't open an iPhone's share sheet. A 10-second iPhone screen recording of
  tapping Share Summary would cover it. Otherwise I'll draw a simple share sheet.
- **The NWS alert is live weather.** Whether a real alert sits over the demo facilities depends on capture day. If
  none does, the opening uses the radar loop and the incident circle.
- **Order of work:** fact-check, then the final script, then the Descript AI voice, then I time the picture to it.
