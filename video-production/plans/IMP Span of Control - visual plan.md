## How each kind of visual gets made

- **One clip per scene (Claude).** Every scene I own gets one MP4, exactly the scene's length (4K, 30 fps, named like `S31 Communication.mp4`), so it drops in and fills the scene. Cuts happen inside the clip, on Rick's words, so a 22-second scene can still change visuals every 4 to 6 seconds. A visual that carries across several scenes is rendered once and cut at your scene breaks, so no scene breaks need removing. If a scene moves in an edit, I re-cut only that clip.
- **Footage inside my clips:** free-licence stock (Pexels; needs a free API key), public-domain military footage from DVIDS for the Marines, and AI shots from OpenArt matched to the kitchen look. I check every shot frame by frame and grade them to match.
- **Descript catalog shots and GIFs:** used only where one catalog shot can fill a whole scene, so you drop one item per scene and never layer inside a scene; the plan gives the search keywords. Where a scene mixes a graphic with footage, I source the footage and cut it into my clip. Inside my clips a GIF beat is a labelled placeholder you swap.
- **Your notes, as design rules:** nothing important in the bottom third (the caption gradient); no small details like the pilot 3 pill; titles open big and centred, then move to their place; social-media motion (punch-ins, kinetic type, a change every 4 to 6 seconds, like the reference); the visual idea changes every scene or two, even when a template is reused.

**Types:** MG = motion graphic (Claude). FOOT-C = footage Claude sources and cuts into the scene clip. FOOT-D = Descript catalog (keywords given). AI = generated shot (Claude, OpenArt). GIF = Descript catalog (keywords). EXIST = your asset or one already placed.
